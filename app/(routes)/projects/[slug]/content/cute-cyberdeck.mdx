## Concept

The hyperfeminine cyberdeck community felt immediately relevant to me, not only as a research subject but as a space I wanted to be part of. Building my own deck was a way of joining that conversation through making. It was also my first physical computing project. The clutch is not just an enclosure. It is the concept.

## Build

- **Hardware:** Raspberry Pi 4, Waveshare 5 inch capacitive touchscreen, mini wireless keyboard with touchpad, flat ribbon cables for HDMI and USB touch, second HDMI output for a beamer
- **Mounts:** Designed in Blender with exact millimeter measurements and 3D printed. The Pi mount carries a snake-like "ssss" pattern. The small letters were rebuilt by hand with modeling gel and finished with chrome gel nail polish.
- **Software:** Raspberry Pi OS, VS Code, Git, Obsidian, Pure Data, Chromium, offline Wikipedia (Kiwix), Organic Maps, SuperTuxKart and the retro RPG Moonring via Box64

## Theoretical Background

The build is connected to my short paper *Cuteness as a Mechanism of Access: The Hyperfeminine Cyberdeck Community as a Case Study in Maker Culture* (Academic Conference Simulation, Film University Babelsberg, 2026), which draws on Cute Studies and feminist HCI to ask how a playful, cute aesthetic can lower the barriers to hardware participation.

## Reflection

The hardest part was finding the hardware: the planned Pi Zero 2 W was sold out for months, so a Pi 4 became the stand-in. Getting openFrameworks to run on the Pi is still an open task. Next steps are a Pi Zero 2 W with a battery for a fully wireless deck, better cooling, and audio-reactive visuals for parties. Most importantly, I lost the fear of touching hardware.
